Fencing options and costs in Burnaby

Fencing in Burnaby is shaped by a lot of everyday realities: homeowners want curb appeal and privacy, but they also need a fence that can handle Lower Mainland–Southwest weather—mild winters with periodic heavy rain, plus wind-driven wear near open corridors. Burnaby has a lot of single-detached homes (18.9% of dwellings), and each detached property is a likely fencing customer for backyards, side yards, and corner lots. With 61,185 homeowner households (60.5% of households own), the local demand is steady and you’ll usually find multiple contractors who can service routine backyard fencing—especially around Metrotown and the Brentwood area, where infill and older homes create constant upgrade work. (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census)

On cost, the Lower Mainland–Southwest “mild but not trivial” frost and wind conditions mean posts can’t always be installed the absolute minimum way. Crews typically set posts deeper than a simplistic coastal rule-of-thumb, and wetter soils around the Fraser Valley often mean deeper holes, more concrete for stability, and more labour time than a dry-site installation. That’s why the same material can land at the low end or high end of a regional band depending on access, slope, and whether you’re adding a gate. Chain-link typically stays lowest-cost; wood sits mid-range; vinyl/PVC and composite are higher because of structure and materials; and aluminum is usually the premium option due to fabrication and finish.

What affects the price of fencing in Burnaby

In Burnaby and across the Lower Mainland–Southwest, fencing quotes for the “same” job can easily swing by 30–50% because of site conditions and how materials perform under local weather. A longer fence line, deeper post setting, more gate work, and panel reinforcement can all add up quickly—and they don’t always show up until the contractor measures the site. In British Columbia, wetter soils and wind exposure can also push crews to stronger framing and more concrete than you’d see on a drier property.

Post depth is the biggest technical driver. Frost still matters: contractors typically set posts about 4–6 inches below the design frost depth, which in practice often means deeper embedment than “surface” installs. In colder parts of Canada, you may hear of 1.5–2.0 m embedment (with concrete setting and longer posts), and while coastal BC tends to be shallower than Ontario and Alberta, Burnaby crews still commonly go deeper than bare minimums to limit heaving in freeze-thaw and to keep gates aligned. Wind exposure is another local factor: even though BC isn’t “Prairie-windy,” coastal and open areas can create constant lateral load, so spacing and post size choices can change mid-quote.

Concrete examples from Burnaby pricing: (1) a backyard that needs deeper holes due to consistently wet soil can add labour and concrete volume; (2) properties with restricted access—tight lanes, limited parking, or landscaping beds—often require more careful removal and re-staging, increasing installation time; (3) an older fence removal with embedded rails can raise disposal and haul-off costs. On the finish side, a wood privacy fence can land in the mid band (often aligned to the $55–$95 range), while switching to vinyl/PVC typically moves you toward the higher end (commonly within the $75–$130 range) because of material cost and structural hardware requirements.

As a rule of thumb, newer homes may still have mature landscaping, but many Burnaby properties built before 1981 (35.5% of homes) often have older fence posts and buried hardware that complicate removal, which is one reason you’ll sometimes see higher disposal and repair line-items. (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census)

Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, fences can be subject to permits and bylaws depending on height and location. As a practical threshold, fences above about 2.0 m (6.5 ft) at the rear and about 1.0–1.2 m (3–4 ft) at the front or corner often trigger the need for a building permit. Even when a permit isn’t required, your fence must respect property lines and municipal setback requirements. Before any post holes are dug, you should have utilities located using the appropriate province-wide service (BC 1 Call in British Columbia). For peace of mind—and to avoid encroachment disputes—you’ll also want a survey or at least a professional property pin locate so you know where the line-fence agreement boundary truly is.

For shared fences, the ownership and responsibilities around line fences are governed by provincial line-fences legislation. When you’re verifying a contractor in Burnaby, ask for proof of insurance and proper coverage before work starts. Step-by-step: (1) verify the contractor’s business/registration details through the appropriate public registry listing for British Columbia; (2) request a certificate of liability insurance naming you as the certificate holder if available; (3) ask for proof of coverage for workers—coverage is typically handled through the WSBC system for eligible employers in BC. If the contractor can’t clearly provide documents and reference numbers, treat it as a red flag.

Concrete “permit needed” items are: fence height beyond typical thresholds and any situation where bylaws require approval before installation. Typically, routine low-height side-yard fences within standard limits (and not affecting setbacks) often don’t require the same level of permitting, but your contractor should confirm based on your location and planned height.

Choosing the right fence material for Burnaby

Choosing the right fence in Burnaby is mostly about matching material to your priorities: privacy, low maintenance, budget, and lifespan—while considering Lower Mainland–Southwest conditions like persistent moisture, freeze-thaw cycles, and coastal wind. Pressure-treated pine is usually the budget option and looks good when new, but in damp BC it will weather and grey faster if you skip upkeep; plan on staining or sealing every 2–3 years. Cedar offers better natural resistance to rot and tends to age more gracefully, but it still benefits from periodic protection to keep it attractive.

Vinyl/PVC is the “set it and forget it” choice: it doesn’t require painting and resists moisture well. The trade-off is cost and the need for correct installation—posts still must be set properly for stability, and extreme cold (rare but real) can affect flexibility, so framing and expansion considerations matter. Chain-link is the lowest upfront material and often the longest-life option, but privacy is limited unless you add privacy slats, which can change airflow and sightline performance. Aluminum ornamental gives a clean, rust-resistant look with minimal maintenance, but it’s not designed for high privacy.

Post-depth decisions still matter across materials. Even though coastal frost is less severe than interior provinces, contractors commonly set posts below frost influence to reduce heaving and keep gates operating smoothly; concrete-set posts are commonly used for corner posts, end posts, and gate posts, especially in wetter soils.

Where the price difference is justified: if you’re choosing between wood and vinyl for a privacy run, a vinyl/PVC option commonly falls into the $75–$130 range, while wood often aligns with the $55–$95 range. If you want to avoid frequent staining and you expect the fence to be there for decades, the added upfront cost can pencil out. But if you’re budget-focused and comfortable with maintenance, pressure-treated pine can make sense—just don’t treat it as “no maintenance” in Burnaby’s wet climate.

How to choose a fencing contractor in Burnaby

Choosing the right contractor in Burnaby comes down to proof and process: licensing/registration, insurance, transparent quoting, and a clear workmanship plan. In British Columbia, verify that your fencing contractor is properly registered for the work they’re doing and provide proof of liability insurance before scheduling. Ask for (1) a certificate of liability insurance (and confirm your address/project is covered as appropriate), (2) proof of workers’ coverage through WSBC where applicable, and (3) documentation that their team is insured to work on occupied residential sites. If they can’t produce documents quickly, you’re taking an unnecessary risk.

Next, get 2–3 itemised written quotes—never a vague lump sum. The quote should separate labour and materials (posts, rails, brackets, fasteners, privacy panels/slats, gate hardware) and note extras such as removal, hauling, and concrete quantities. Read the scope line-by-line: what’s excluded (rock excavation, stump grinding, trimming plants), is permit support included (where applicable), and is disposal included or charged separately? A proper warranty should specify workmanship length (how long the installer backs the build), product/manufacturer warranty, and whether the warranty transfers to you if you sell the home.

Payment schedule matters: never pay more than about 10–15% upfront. Use a holdback until the final inspection is complete and gate operation/privacy alignment is verified. Finally, ask for a written start date and completion estimate; fencing projects in Burnaby can be scheduled around wet-weather days, material lead times, and neighbour coordination.

Frequently asked questions — fencing in Burnaby

Can I install a fence on a slope in Burnaby?

Yes—many fences in Burnaby are built on slopes, especially in older neighbourhoods and along drainage-affected backyards. The key is how the fence is stepped or racked: panels can be installed with consistent footing lines by stepping the rails, or you can use raked designs that follow the grade. A good contractor will also account for how the slope changes post depths and whether concrete setting is needed to keep the fence stable. Because Burnaby soil can stay wet, deeper post holes and proper concrete at corner and gate posts help prevent lean over time. If you need a permit due to height or corner location, plan the layout before digging so you don’t have to rework panels.

What is the most weather-resistant fence for Burnaby's climate?

“Most weather-resistant” usually means the best combination of moisture tolerance, rot resistance, and stable installation. In Burnaby and the Lower Mainland–Southwest, vinyl/PVC and properly installed cedar tend to perform well because they resist wet conditions better than untreated or poorly finished wood. Vinyl/PVC is popular for low maintenance and often lands in the $75–$130 installed range per linear foot depending on style and hardware. Cedar can be very durable and attractive, but it still needs periodic protection to avoid finish breakdown. Chain-link is also highly durable against rain and works well long-term, but it won’t give privacy unless you add slats or screening. Whatever you choose, stable posts (set below frost influence where needed) and correct gate bracing are what make “weather resistance” real.

How do I prevent wood fence rot in Burnaby?

To prevent rot in Burnaby’s wet, freeze-thaw environment, focus on the “water management” details: use properly treated pressure-treated lumber or cedar intended for exterior use, and ensure posts are set correctly with drainage-aware installation. Concrete-set posts at corners and gate points are common because they keep alignment and reduce soil contact and wobble. Avoid sealing wood where water can get trapped; the goal is to keep fasteners and end grain protected while allowing the fence to dry between wet periods. For maintenance, plan on staining or sealing on a schedule—pressure-treated pine often needs attention every 2–3 years if you want to avoid fast grey weathering and premature deterioration. Finally, keep bottom rails slightly elevated where possible and trim nearby vegetation so air can move through the fence line.

What is the cost per linear foot for fencing in Burnaby?

Burnaby fencing is commonly priced using material category ranges. As a starting point, chain-link is often the lowest at roughly $30–$55 per linear foot installed, while wood typically lands around $55–$95 per linear foot depending on height, privacy style, and post setting requirements. Vinyl/PVC is usually higher—often within the $75–$130 range—because of material and structural hardware. Aluminum ornamental is usually at the top end of the typical residential bands, often around $90–$170 per linear foot. Your final figure depends heavily on fence length, gate count, slope, and whether old fence removal/disposal and permit-related work are included. For an accurate number, request itemised quotes with labour and materials separated.

Should I repair or replace my old fence in Burnaby?

Often you can repair, but it depends on post stability and how deep the moisture damage goes. If the posts are still straight and the rot is limited to a few boards or rails, repairs can be cost-effective. However, if posts are soft, leaning, or the bottom rails are failing in multiple sections, partial repairs can turn into a “keep patching” situation—especially on wet sites where hidden rot develops around post bases. Also consider whether hardware corrosion has loosened connections. A replacement may be smarter if you’re upgrading privacy, adding gates, or aligning the fence to a clearer property line. In Burnaby, older homes (many built before 1981) can have buried components that increase removal time and disposal costs, so it’s worth getting an itemised quote that explicitly addresses removal, concrete setting, and post replacement numbers.

How do I handle a fence dispute with a neighbour in British Columbia?

Fence disputes in British Columbia are usually about boundary location, shared responsibilities, and workmanship expectations. Start by clarifying the actual property line: locate your pins/survey markers before any major changes. If you share a line fence, provincial line-fences legislation can govern what each side is responsible for, so it’s worth reviewing your situation rather than relying on informal agreements. Document everything—photos, measurements, and written communication—and communicate through calm, factual terms like fence height, material choice, and who will pay for what. Before digging, use BC 1 Call for utility locates. If you and your neighbour can’t agree, consider a mediator or professional survey resolution rather than forcing construction. For the practical steps, ask contractors to provide a written scope that matches the agreed boundary and includes permit/height details when relevant in Burnaby.
